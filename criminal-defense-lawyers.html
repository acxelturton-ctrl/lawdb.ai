Criminal Defense Lawyers in the United States

  • Updated November 16, 2026
  • 2 min read
  • General information, not legal advice

A criminal defense lawyer represents people who have been arrested or charged with a crime, from DUI and drug charges to theft, assault and serious felonies. They explain the charges and your rights, deal with the prosecutor, and defend you in court if the case goes to trial. The earlier a lawyer is involved, the more options there usually are.

When to speak to a criminal defense lawyer

  • You have been arrested, charged or told you are under investigation.
  • Police want to question you, or you have received a court date or summons.
  • You were charged with DUI or another driving offense and your license is at risk.
  • You want to know whether a past record can be sealed or expunged.
  • You are a non-citizen and a charge could affect your immigration status.

What a criminal defense lawyer does

  • Explains the charges, the possible outcomes and your rights.
  • Speaks with the police and the prosecutor on your behalf.
  • Challenges the evidence and negotiates, where appropriate, for a reduced charge or dismissal.
  • Represents you at hearings, at trial and at sentencing.
  • Advises on bail, probation and sealing or expunging a record.

How the rules differ by state

Criminal law is mostly state law, with its own offenses, courts and procedures in each state, and federal charges are handled in separate federal courts. Rules on DUI penalties, bail and record sealing differ a lot from state to state, so hire a lawyer who regularly defends cases in the county or court where you were charged.

Questions to ask before you hire a criminal defense lawyer

  1. How often do you handle cases like mine in this court?
  2. What are the realistic outcomes, and what does each one involve?
  3. Who will actually work on my case, and how will you keep me informed?
  4. How do you bill, and what is included?

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I am arrested?

You have the right to remain silent and the right to a lawyer. Politely say that you want a lawyer and do not answer questions about the case until you have spoken to one. Do not discuss the matter with others, and do not sign anything you do not understand.

Can I get a public defender?

If you are charged with a crime that could lead to jail and you cannot afford a lawyer, the court will generally appoint one. Many people also choose to hire a private defense lawyer, who may be able to give a case more individual time. Either way, the lawyer's duty to you is the same.

Can criminal charges be reduced or dismissed?

Sometimes. It depends on the evidence, how it was gathered, the facts of the case and the prosecutor's position. A lawyer can tell you what is realistic after reviewing the details.
